Conflict Analysis

The central issue is not one message

The latest job-opening message is not the core injury by itself. The broader injury is the combination of prior relationship history, unclear disclosure, continued communication after marriage, deleted/recovered messages, conflict-sharing with Kevin, resumed casual contact after boundaries, and contact being discovered rather than disclosed.

Kevin appears to have been treated as an exception

A normal friendship typically would not require aliases, hidden marital context, conflict explanations, direct boundary messages, repeated post-boundary contact, secure-account concern, or passcode changes.

“Bro” language does not erase the history

Casual language can still function as a low-risk re-entry point. The concern is not only wording; it is continued access after boundaries.

The conflict has been reactive, not repair-first

The pattern appears discovery-driven: something is found, confrontation happens, explanation follows, boundary is debated, then another discovery occurs.

Core question: “Why is this door still open after everything this has cost our marriage?”
